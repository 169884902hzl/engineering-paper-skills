The view-checking policy achieved the highest tabletop sorting success among the evaluated camera and rechecking variants. Across 120 trials, the full policy completed 81% of trials successfully, compared with 70% for a fixed front camera, 67% for a fixed wrist camera, and 58% when label readings were not rechecked before placement. The ordering of these results is consistent with the observed failure modes: the front camera could miss labels when packages were tilted, while the wrist camera provided close-up label views but lost the wider bin context needed for placement. Removing the recheck stage produced the lowest success rate, indicating that early label-reading errors were often propagated directly into the final placement decision. These results support view checking as a useful component for tabletop sorting under the tested conditions, limited here to one lab table and three package types, with no confidence intervals, significance test, or deployment evaluation.
