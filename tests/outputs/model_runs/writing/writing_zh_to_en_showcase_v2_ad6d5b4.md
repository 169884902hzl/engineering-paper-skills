Occlusion becomes the dominant failure source during the final approach to peg-hole insertion, where the peg itself reduces the information available to a fixed overhead camera and converts early pose error into contact failure under open-loop execution. The proposed system treats this loss of visibility as part of the insertion problem: overhead and side RGB-D observations are first used to estimate the insertion pose and an associated confidence, a low-confidence estimate triggers acquisition of an additional view and pose update, and the final insertion is executed under force and pose-deviation guards. Across 180 real-robot trials, the full system achieved an 84% success rate, outperforming fixed-overhead sensing at 69%, fixed-side sensing at 72%, and open-loop execution at 61%. This ranking is consistent with the supplied failure modes: overhead sensing provides global context early but is vulnerable near contact, side sensing preserves lateral alignment cues but leaves depth less stable, and open-loop control carries perception error directly into the contact phase. These results are descriptive for one 7-DoF arm, one tabletop fixture, and one cylindrical peg family, without statistical significance testing or cross-robot, deployment, or industrial evaluation.
