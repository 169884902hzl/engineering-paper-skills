The full system achieved the highest success rate, reaching 84% over 180 trials, compared with 69% for the fixed overhead view, 72% for the fixed side view, and 61% for open-loop execution. The ranking reflects how each variant carries uncertainty into the final phase of the task. The overhead-only setup tends to lose the hole near the end of execution, while the side view preserves lateral relation but leaves depth estimates less reliable. Open-loop execution performs worst because the estimated pose is not updated before contact, so early pose error propagates directly into the insertion attempt. These results indicate that the main benefit comes from maintaining usable pose information through the late execution stage, rather than from a single fixed viewpoint alone. The evidence is limited to one tabletop setup and one cylindrical peg family, with no statistical test, cross-robot evaluation, or deployment study.
