Contact-rich robotic insertion under partial visual occlusion is difficult because the visual cues for peg-hole alignment degrade as the robot approaches contact. When a fixed view loses the relevant alignment cue, a small pose error can be carried into contact, where open-loop execution has limited opportunity to recover. We propose confidence-triggered multi-view insertion, a system that treats insertion pose and confidence as the state to be updated before contact-rich execution. The system estimates pose and confidence from overhead and side RGB-D observations, requests an additional view when confidence is low, and refines the pose estimate before continuing. The refined estimate is executed by a guarded insertion controller that stops when measured force or pose deviation exceeds the specified limits. In 180 real-robot trials on one 7-DoF arm, one tabletop fixture, and one cylindrical peg family, the system achieved 84% success compared with 69% for fixed overhead, 72% for fixed side, and 61% for open-loop baselines, without claiming statistical significance, cross-robot generalization, or deployment readiness.
