The comparison suggests that load-adaptive gain scheduling makes the cable-driven stage less sensitive to changes in payload, but does not remove the load-dependent failure mode. Controller B reduced the mean positioning error from 1.8 mm to 1.2 mm under the nominal load and from 3.9 mm to 2.6 mm under the heavy load, indicating a consistent reduction across the two tested conditions. The larger errors under the heavy load show that the stage remains harder to regulate as load increases, while the similar relative improvement suggests that the adaptive gains compensate for part of this change rather than solving it completely. This interpretation is also consistent with the observed overshoot during fast reversals under heavy loading, where transient motion still exceeds the controller’s effective operating envelope. These results support the use of load-adaptive control for improving empirical positioning accuracy on the tested prototype, with the present boundary limited to one stage, two load settings, and laboratory evaluation; frequency-domain stability, fatigue behavior, long-duration drift, and production reliability remain untested.
