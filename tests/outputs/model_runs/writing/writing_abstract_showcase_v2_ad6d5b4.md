Contact-rich peg insertion becomes difficult under partial visual occlusion because alignment cues deteriorate near contact, allowing residual pose error to enter the phase in which lateral jamming and force-limited recovery are least forgiving. We introduce confidence-triggered multi-view insertion, which treats the insertion pose and its confidence as the state to be updated before committing to contact-rich execution. The system estimates pose and confidence from overhead and side RGB-D observations, requests an additional view when confidence is low, and refines the pose estimate before motion execution. A guarded insertion controller then converts the refined estimate into action while stopping on force or pose-deviation limits, limiting the effect of errors that remain after perception. On one 7-DoF robot arm, one tabletop fixture, and one cylindrical peg family, the method achieved 84% success across 180 real-robot trials, compared with 69% for a fixed overhead view, 72% for a fixed side view, and 61% for an open-loop baseline. These results indicate that confidence-gated viewpoint acquisition can reduce occlusion-driven insertion failures in this bounded tabletop setting, with remaining failures concentrated in fully ambiguous views and large contact-induced pose deviations.
