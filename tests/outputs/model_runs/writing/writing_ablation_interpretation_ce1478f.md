Within the evaluated task family, the ablation results suggest that reliability improves when action hints are progressively tied to task-relevant visual and geometric constraints. Geometry-aware execution alone raises success only modestly, from 17% to 23%, indicating that execution constraints help but cannot compensate for weak target selection. Adding target-focused perception produces a larger gain to 44%, suggesting that the system benefits substantially when the action is conditioned on the intended target rather than the scene as a whole. The largest increase, from 44% to 76%, occurs when overlay self-verification is added, which supports its role in rejecting or correcting visually plausible but poorly grounded actions. The final increase to 88% with mask-constrained grounding suggests that tighter spatial grounding further reduces ambiguity. These trends support the roles of the components in this task family, but without statistical significance testing, isolated component swaps, or deployment evaluation, they should not be read as proof of a specific causal mechanism or as evidence of real-world robustness beyond the tested setting.
