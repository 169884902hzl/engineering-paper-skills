Contact-rich robotic insertion under partial visual occlusion is difficult not only because the robot must estimate where a peg meets a hole, but because the information needed for that estimate changes as the motion approaches contact. At the start of an insertion attempt, overhead or side observations may still reveal enough of the fixture to initialize an alignment hypothesis. Near the final approach, however, the peg can occlude the hole, the visible geometry can become less diagnostic, and the pose estimate that was adequate for free-space motion can become unreliable exactly when correction becomes harder. The core bottleneck is therefore the coupling between visual ambiguity and contact commitment: if the robot continues from an uncertain pose estimate, open-loop motion can carry that uncertainty into contact, where small alignment errors are more difficult to recover safely.

Common insertion routes address only parts of this coupled problem. Fixed overhead and side RGB-D views improve coverage, but their visibility is chosen before the geometry changes and cannot actively recover the task-relevant cue once occlusion dominates. Open-loop insertion keeps execution simple and efficient, but it assumes that the pose estimate remains usable after contact begins. Force-guarded control can stop unsafe motion, yet stopping does not resolve the upstream visual ambiguity that produced the uncertain insertion pose. These gaps motivate a confidence-triggered multi-view insertion system that estimates insertion pose and confidence from overhead and side RGB-D observations, requests an additional view when confidence is low, refines the estimate before continuing, and executes the insertion with force and pose-deviation stops within the tested tabletop setting.
