Because pose uncertainty that survives into contact is difficult to correct safely, the method first resolves the insertion pose in perception and only then commits to guarded execution. From the overhead RGB-D observation, the side RGB-D observation, and the candidate insertion pose with its confidence estimate, the system forms the intermediate object used for downstream control: an insertion pose estimate paired with a confidence score. This pairing makes the handoff explicit, since the controller receives a pose only after the visual evidence has been checked for whether it is sufficient under partial occlusion. When the confidence remains low, the system requests an additional view and uses the new RGB-D observation to update the insertion pose before physical execution begins. The refined pose estimate is then passed to the guarded insertion controller, which executes insertion from that estimate while stopping on force or pose-deviation limits. This ordering keeps uncertain visual reasoning upstream of contact and confines the execution phase to bounded guarded motion rather than using contact to repair unresolved pose ambiguity.
