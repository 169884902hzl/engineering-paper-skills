In the real-robot insertion trials, the central failure mode was not initial object recognition but the loss of usable hole visibility near contact, when the peg itself increasingly occluded the target geometry. Fixed overhead sensing captured the global layout early but degraded in the final approach, while the side view preserved lateral alignment cues without fully resolving depth ambiguity. The proposed system uses both RGB-D views to estimate the insertion pose and its confidence, requests an additional view when the estimate is uncertain, and then executes insertion under force and pose-deviation guards. In a 180-trial evaluation, this closed-loop, confidence-triggered system achieved an 84% success rate, compared with 69% for fixed overhead sensing, 72% for fixed side sensing, and 61% for open-loop execution. The gap to the open-loop baseline is consistent with the role of late pose updates: early pose errors that would otherwise be carried directly into contact can be corrected before guarded insertion. These results characterize one 7-DoF robot arm, one tabletop fixture, and one cylindrical peg family, with no statistical significance test or cross-robot, deployment, or industrial evaluation.
