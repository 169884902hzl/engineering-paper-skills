We presented confidence-triggered multi-view insertion for contact-rich robotic insertion under partial occlusion. The system estimates insertion pose and confidence from overhead and side RGB-D observations, requests an additional view when confidence is low, refines the pose estimate, and executes guarded insertion with force and pose-deviation stops. In the tested real-robot protocol, the full system achieved 84% success over 180 trials, compared with 69% for fixed overhead viewing, 72% for fixed side viewing, and 61% for open-loop execution. Because the current evidence does not isolate view selection from guarded execution, the result is best interpreted at the system level. Taken together, these results support the system-level value of confidence-triggered multi-view insertion in the tested tabletop setting, not isolated causal proof of view selection or guarded execution.

The remaining failures identify the current operating boundary. The method can still fail when all available views are ambiguous, leaving the pose estimate without a reliable visual update, or when contact produces a pose deviation large enough for guarded execution to stop the attempt. Future work should therefore test recovery from fully ambiguous viewing conditions and contact-induced pose deviation, rather than extending claims beyond the single 7-DoF arm, tabletop fixture, and cylindrical peg family evaluated here.
