The full system retrieves tools successfully in 73% of 150 trials, compared with 52% for tray-level proposal alone and 64% when wrist-camera verification is added. The 12-point gain from wrist verification indicates that a second, closer view filters some proposal errors caused by reflective tool handles, rather than leaving the tray-level proposal as the only decision point. However, verification does not remove failures that occur after visual confirmation: thin tools can still pass the wrist-camera check and then slip during the guarded lift. The final gain to 73% therefore reflects the value of coupling proposal, verification, and lift checking, while the evaluation remains bounded to two tray layouts and six tool categories, with no significance test, factory deployment, or multi-robot evaluation.
