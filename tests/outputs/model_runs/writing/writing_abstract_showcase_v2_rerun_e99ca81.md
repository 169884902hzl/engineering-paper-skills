In contact-rich peg insertion, peg-hole alignment cues can degrade near contact, allowing a fixed or open-loop policy to carry pose error into the least recoverable part of the motion. We introduce confidence-triggered multi-view insertion, which represents the insertion pose and its confidence as the state to be updated before contact-rich execution. The system estimates pose and confidence from overhead and side RGB-D observations, requests an additional view when confidence is low, refines the pose estimate, and then hands the result to a guarded insertion controller. The controller executes the insertion while stopping on force or pose-deviation limits, so perception updates are concentrated before the part of the task where recovery is most constrained. Across 180 real-robot trials, the method achieved 84% success, compared with 69% for a fixed overhead baseline, 72% for a fixed side baseline, and 61% for an open-loop baseline. This evaluation characterizes the method on one 7-DoF arm, one tabletop fixture, and one cylindrical peg family, with remaining failures in fully ambiguous views and large contact-induced pose deviation.
