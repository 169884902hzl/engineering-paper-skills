The full system reaches 84% success over 180 trials on contact-rich peg insertion under partial visual occlusion, outperforming the fixed overhead camera, fixed side camera, and open-loop controller settings at 69%, 72%, and 61%, respectively. The ranking reflects the visibility limits of each observation and execution strategy: the overhead view loses the hole near final approach, the side view preserves lateral visibility but leaves depth uncertain, and open-loop execution carries pose error into contact. Combining view-conditioned grounding with constrained execution therefore improves success by reducing the pose ambiguity that remains most harmful near insertion.

The additive ablation shows where the performance gains enter the pipeline. A naive direct action hint reaches 17%, and geometry-aware execution alone raises success only to 23%, indicating that execution constraints have limited value when the target is poorly grounded. Adding target-focused perception increases success to 44%, overlay self-verification raises it further to 76%, and full mask-constrained grounding reaches 88%, suggesting that the largest gains come from progressively narrowing and checking the target estimate before execution. Because the rows are additive rather than factorial, these results support the role of the combined pipeline rather than isolating independent component effects.

The remaining failures define the current operating envelope of the system. The evaluation covers one 7-DoF robot arm, one tabletop fixture, and one cylindrical peg family, so the results characterize this controlled insertion setting rather than cross-robot deployment or broader object families. Failures persist when all available views remain ambiguous or when contact produces a pose deviation too large for the grounded execution policy to recover within the evaluated tabletop setup.
