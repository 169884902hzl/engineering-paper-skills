We presented a verification-driven dual-arm manipulation framework for manipulating target objects from a single RGB-D observation. The framework combines target-focused perception, structured action-hint generation, iterative verification, mask-constrained grounding, and geometry-aware execution to connect visual input with executable dual-arm actions. In the reported evaluation, the full system achieved an average success rate of 88%, compared with 19% for the best trained baseline. Ablations showing reduced success after removing verification and grounding further support the role of these components, while robustness tests across appearance, diameter, and complexity variations indicate stable performance within the tested conditions.

The current results remain bounded by the evaluated setting. Very thin objects and severe occlusion remain difficult, and the experiments were conducted on one real robot platform without industrial deployment evaluation. Future work should therefore focus on improving perception and grounding for thin or occluded targets, and on testing whether the framework maintains its performance across additional robot platforms and application conditions.
