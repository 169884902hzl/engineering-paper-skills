Our system separates target localization from contact execution to keep uncertainty management in the perception stage. Given overhead and wrist RGB-D observations, the perception module first estimates the target pose together with a confidence score, rather than passing image observations directly to the robot controller. When this estimate is reliable, the pose is handed to a guarded insertion controller; when confidence is low, the system requests an additional side view and updates the target pose from the new observation before attempting insertion. This ordering treats viewpoint selection as part of pose estimation, so ambiguous geometry is resolved while the robot is still outside contact. After the pose is selected, execution remains conservative: the controller performs guarded insertion and terminates the motion if the force limit is reached or if the robot deviates from the expected pose. Resolving perception uncertainty before contact reduces the cost of correction, because additional sensing can revise the target estimate without requiring recovery from a partially executed insertion.
