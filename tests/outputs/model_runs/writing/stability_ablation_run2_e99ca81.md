In the additive ablation, the main bottleneck is target disambiguation rather than action execution alone. A naive direct action hint succeeds in only 17% of trials, and adding geometry-aware execution raises success to 23%, indicating that better execution helps realize an action but is insufficient when the correct insertion target remains visually ambiguous. Introducing target-focused perception increases success to 44%, showing that narrowing the visual evidence around the intended target removes a substantial portion of the scene-level ambiguity. The largest gain appears when overlay self-verification is added, raising success from 44% to 76%; this step contributes by rejecting action hints that are visually plausible but poorly grounded before execution. The full system reaches 88% with mask-constrained grounding, which further reduces spatial ambiguity near the target. Thus, the ablation assigns complementary roles to the stages: execution stabilizes realization, target-focused perception selects the relevant evidence, self-verification filters groundedness failures, and mask constraints refine local grounding. Since the rows are additive within one task family, the results support this contribution-level interpretation rather than isolated causal or statistically tested component effects.
