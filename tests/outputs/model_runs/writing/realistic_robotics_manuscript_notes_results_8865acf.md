The table reports 84% insertion success over 180 trials for confidence-triggered multi-view insertion, compared with 72% for a fixed wrist-side camera, 69% for a fixed overhead camera, and 61% for open-loop pose execution. The ranking suggests that the gain is not only from adding another camera view, but from updating the view when the relevant geometric evidence changes during approach. The overhead view observes the fixture early, yet loses the hole near the final approach; the wrist-side view preserves the lateral peg-hole relationship, but still leaves depth uncertain. By using confidence to decide when the pose estimate should be refreshed, the proposed method recovers part of the information lost by either fixed-view strategy and avoids committing fully to the initial open-loop pose. The remaining failures occur when all available views are ambiguous or when contact changes the pose after execution has begun, so the present result supports the evaluated one-arm, one-fixture, one-peg-family setting rather than a broader robustness claim.

Missing-evidence note: confidence intervals, statistical tests, baseline trial counts, and results across additional robots, fixtures, or peg families are still needed before claiming robustness or general deployment performance.
