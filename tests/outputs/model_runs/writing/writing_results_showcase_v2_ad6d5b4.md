In contact-rich peg insertion, the best-performing policy is the one that prevents visual ambiguity from becoming unrecovered contact error. The confidence-triggered multi-view system achieved 84% success over 180 trials, outperforming fixed side viewing by 12 percentage points, fixed overhead viewing by 15 percentage points, and open-loop execution by 23 percentage points. This ranking follows the diagnostic failure modes: the overhead camera loses the hole during the final approach when the peg occludes it, whereas the side camera preserves lateral visibility but still leaves depth uncertainty unresolved. Open-loop execution performs worst because pose errors estimated before contact are carried into insertion without a recovery mechanism. By requesting an additional view only when confidence is low and then executing guarded contact, the full system reduces both perception-side ambiguity and contact-side error propagation. Remaining failures occur when all available views remain ambiguous or contact generates a large pose deviation. The demonstrated scope is a coupled multi-view and guarded-execution system on one 7-DoF arm, one tabletop fixture, and one cylindrical peg family.
