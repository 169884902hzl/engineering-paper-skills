As a peg approaches a hole, the visual evidence needed for insertion can disappear at the moment when corrective motion becomes most constrained. In a tabletop RGB-D setup, an overhead view may provide an early estimate of the hole center, but the peg itself can occlude that cue during the final approach. A side view can preserve lateral visibility, yet it may leave depth uncertainty unresolved. Once the motion enters contact, open-loop execution simply carries this uncertainty forward, while force guards can stop unsafe interaction without explaining which pose estimate should have been trusted. The insertion problem therefore changes across execution stages: the relevant question is not only whether the scene can be perceived accurately in one view, but whether the robot can recognize when its current visual estimate is no longer sufficient before contact narrows the recovery margin.

We formulate confidence-triggered multi-view insertion around this stage-dependent ambiguity. The system estimates both an insertion pose and its confidence from overhead and side RGB-D observations, requests an additional view when confidence is low, and refines the pose before executing the insertion under force and pose-deviation stops. This formulation couples perception timing to estimated uncertainty rather than committing to a fixed view or relying on contact feedback to compensate for unresolved visual ambiguity. In a single 7-DoF robot arm setup with one tabletop fixture and a cylindrical peg family, the full system reaches 84% success over 180 trials, compared with 69% for fixed overhead sensing, 72% for fixed side sensing, and 61% for open-loop execution. The remaining failures occur when all available views remain ambiguous or when contact induces large pose deviation, defining the current operating scope of the approach.
