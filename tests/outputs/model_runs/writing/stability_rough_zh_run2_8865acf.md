Peg-in-hole insertion becomes difficult when the visual evidence needed for alignment changes during the approach. The overhead RGB-D view provides an informative initial estimate, but the hole can become hidden by the peg near contact, while the side view exposes lateral alignment at the cost of less stable depth. We therefore estimate pose and confidence jointly from overhead and side RGB-D observations, trigger an additional observation when confidence is low, and update the insertion pose before guarded execution. The final insertion stage uses force and pose-deviation stops to limit execution after contact has begun. Across 180 trials, the full system achieves an 84% success rate, compared with 69% using the overhead view alone, 72% using the side view alone, and 61% for open-loop execution. These results support the value of confidence-triggered multi-view pose updating in the evaluated setup, with evidence limited to one 7-DoF arm, one fixture, and a cylindrical peg family; statistical significance, cross-robot transfer, and deployment conditions are outside the present evaluation.
