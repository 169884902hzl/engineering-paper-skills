Occluded peg-hole insertion concentrates the main perception error at the moment when the robot most needs an updated pose estimate: as the peg approaches the hole, the fixed camera view that initially localizes the fixture loses visibility of the target. In our real-robot evaluation, the full system combines overhead and side RGB-D observations to estimate the insertion pose and its confidence, requests an additional viewpoint when confidence is low, and executes the final motion under force and pose-deviation guards. Across 180 trials on a 7-DoF arm, this closed-loop view-selection and guarded-execution pipeline achieved an 84% success rate, compared with 69% for a fixed overhead camera, 72% for a fixed side camera, and 61% for open-loop execution. The ranking is consistent with the central failure mode of the task: a single overhead view provides broad early context but degrades near contact, while a side view preserves lateral information yet leaves depth less stable; open-loop execution further carries early pose errors directly into contact. These results evaluate one tabletop fixture and one cylindrical peg family, and therefore define the operating evidence for this setup rather than cross-robot, industrial, or statistically tested generalization.
