In the ablation, the dominant bottleneck is not simply executing an insertion action, but grounding that action to the correct visually ambiguous target. The naive direct action hint succeeds in 17% of trials, and geometry-aware execution alone raises success only to 23%, indicating that improved action realization cannot select the target by itself. Adding target-focused perception increases success to 44%, reducing scene-level ambiguity before the execution stage. The largest gain enters with overlay self-verification, which raises success from 44% to 76% by rejecting action hints that look plausible but are poorly grounded in the target evidence. The full system reaches 88% success when mask-constrained grounding is added, consistent with its role in reducing residual spatial ambiguity near the target. Since the rows are additive and evaluated on one task family, the ablation supports these staged contribution roles rather than establishing independent causal effects, statistical significance, broad robustness, or deployment readiness.
