In cluttered workbench trays, adding verification and guarded execution improved retrieval over proposal alone. The proposal-only configuration completed 52% of trials, while adding wrist-camera verification increased success to 64%, indicating that close-range verification helps reject some errors introduced by tray-level proposals. The full system reached 73% success over 150 trials, further separating the combined pipeline from the proposal-only baseline. The remaining failures show why wrist verification is useful but insufficient as a complete reliability mechanism: reflective tool handles still confuse the proposal stage, and thin tools can pass wrist-camera verification yet slip during the lift. Thus, the ablation supports the role of wrist verification as an intermediate check between tray-level perception and execution, while the guarded lift check addresses a different failure regime tied to physical retrieval. These results are bounded to two tray layouts and six tool categories, with no significance test, factory deployment, or multi-robot evaluation.
