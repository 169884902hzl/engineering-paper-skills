Confidence-triggered multi-view insertion links perception confidence to execution in contact-rich peg insertion under partial visual occlusion. By estimating pose and confidence from overhead and side RGB-D observations, requesting another view when confidence is low, refining the insertion pose, and executing with force and pose-deviation stops, the system raises success to 84% over 180 trials, compared with 69% for a fixed overhead camera, 72% for a fixed side camera, and 61% for open-loop control. These results support a bounded takeaway: in the evaluated setup, reliability improves when visual ambiguity is treated as an execution-relevant signal rather than a fixed sensing condition.

All available views remain ambiguous, or contact produces pose deviations beyond guarded recovery, marking the main failure boundary of the current system. This boundary was evaluated on one 7-DoF robot arm, one tabletop fixture, and one cylindrical peg family, with descriptive comparisons but no statistical significance test, cross-robot evaluation, deployment study, or isolated causal separation between view selection and guarded execution. Future work should separate these component roles and test whether the confidence-triggered strategy transfers across broader hardware, fixture, and peg conditions.
