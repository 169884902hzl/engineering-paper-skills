Under partial visual occlusion, success was governed by how much visibility loss and depth uncertainty remained before contact. The confidence-triggered multi-view system achieved 84% success over 180 trials, exceeding the fixed side view by 12 percentage points, the fixed overhead view by 15 percentage points, and open-loop execution by 23 percentage points. This ordering reflects distinct failure modes rather than a simple camera preference: the overhead view loses the hole during final approach when the peg occludes it, while the side view preserves lateral visibility but leaves depth uncertainty unresolved. Open-loop execution performs worst because pose errors estimated before insertion are carried directly into contact, where they are not corrected. By triggering an additional view only under low confidence and then using guarded contact execution, the full system reduces the chance that ambiguous visual evidence becomes an unrecovered contact pose error. The remaining failures occurred when all available views were ambiguous or when contact generated a pose deviation too large for the guarded execution to recover, so the result supports a scoped system-level finding on one 7-DoF arm, one tabletop fixture, and one cylindrical peg family.
