Our system separates pose qualification from insertion execution so that the robot does not commit to an insertion motion immediately after the initial visual estimate. The method takes overhead RGB-D observations, side RGB-D observations, a candidate insertion pose, and its confidence as inputs. Instead of converting this candidate directly into an action, the perception stage produces a refined insertion pose together with an updated confidence value. This intermediate representation forms the handoff between perception and control: when the confidence is sufficient, the refined pose is passed to the insertion controller; when it is insufficient, the system requests an additional viewpoint and updates the pose estimate before execution. The execution stage then performs guarded insertion using the refined pose as the target while monitoring force and pose deviation during the motion. This design makes pose credibility an explicit decision point before guarded execution, while leaving numerical confidence thresholds, latency, calibration procedures, and analytic update formulas outside the present method specification.
