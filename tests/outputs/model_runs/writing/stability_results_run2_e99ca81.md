In contact-rich peg insertion, performance follows how much pose evidence remains when visibility degrades near contact. The confidence-triggered multi-view system achieved 84% success over 180 trials, ranking above the fixed side camera at 72%, the fixed overhead camera at 69%, and the open-loop controller at 61%, with gains of 12, 15, and 23 percentage points, respectively. This ordering reflects the diagnostic failure modes: the overhead-only view loses the hole during final approach when the peg occludes it, whereas the side view preserves lateral visibility but leaves depth uncertainty unresolved. Open-loop execution performs worst because unrecovered pose errors are carried into contact rather than corrected during guarded insertion. Remaining failures occurred when all views were ambiguous or contact produced a large pose deviation, placing the demonstrated operating scope within a single 7-DoF arm, one tabletop fixture, and one cylindrical peg family.
