In the final approach phase, peg-hole insertion becomes limited by visibility loss rather than by the initial detection of the hole: the peg increasingly occludes the target from the overhead RGB-D view, while the side view preserves lateral alignment cues but provides less stable depth. In 180 real-robot trials, the proposed system achieved an 84% insertion success rate, compared with 69% for a fixed overhead view, 72% for a fixed side view, and 61% for open-loop execution. This ranking reflects the role of view-adaptive pose refinement in the central failure mode of the task. The full system first estimates the insertion pose and confidence from overhead and side RGB-D observations, requests an additional view when confidence is low, and updates the pose estimate before contact. The guarded insertion stage then constrains execution using force and pose-deviation checks, reducing the extent to which early pose errors are carried directly into contact. The evaluation covers one 7-DoF robot arm, one tabletop fixture, and one cylindrical peg family; it does not include statistical significance testing, cross-robot evaluation, or deployment in industrial settings.
