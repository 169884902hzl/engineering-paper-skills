Across 180 trials in the tabletop peg-in-hole evaluation, the full system achieved the highest success rate, reaching 84% compared with 72% for the side fixed-camera variant, 69% for the overhead fixed-camera variant, and 61% for open-loop execution. The 12 percentage-point gap over the stronger fixed-view variant suggests that the combined system benefits from complementary visual information: the overhead view tends to lose the hole near the end of insertion, while the side view preserves the lateral relation but leaves depth estimation uncertain. The larger 23 percentage-point gap over open-loop execution indicates that uncorrected pose error remains a major failure source, because open-loop execution carries the error into contact rather than updating the estimate before insertion. These results support the value of closed-loop visual correction for the evaluated cylindrical peg family in a single tabletop setup; cross-robot transfer, statistical significance, and deployment performance remain outside this evaluation.
