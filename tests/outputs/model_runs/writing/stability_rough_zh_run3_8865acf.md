Occlusion becomes most limiting in peg-hole insertion near contact, when the peg can hide the hole from the overhead camera. A side RGB-D view provides complementary lateral information, but its depth estimate is less stable, and open-loop execution carries early pose errors directly into contact. Our approach therefore combines overhead and side RGB-D observations to estimate both insertion pose and confidence; when confidence is low, the system reacquires the target and updates the pose before executing guarded insertion with force and pose-deviation stopping conditions. Across 180 trials, the full system achieved 84% success, compared with 69% for overhead-only perception, 72% for side-only perception, and 61% for open-loop execution. This ranking suggests that the main gain comes from updating the pose before contact rather than relying on either single-view perception or a fixed early estimate. The evaluation is bounded to one 7-DoF arm, one fixture, and a cylindrical peg family; statistical significance, cross-robot transfer, and deployment-scale operation remain outside the present study.
