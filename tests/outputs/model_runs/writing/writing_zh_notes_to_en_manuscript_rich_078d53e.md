Contact-rich robotic insertion fails in this setting because the critical uncertainty is not simply whether the robot can see the scene, but whether the visible cues still support peg-hole alignment as the motion approaches contact. A fixed camera may capture the overall fixture early in the trial while losing the alignment information that matters in the final approach, and an open-loop insertion can then carry an initial pose error into contact where recovery becomes difficult. We therefore formulate insertion around a confidence-aware pose update: overhead and side RGB-D observations are used to estimate the insertion pose and its confidence, an additional view is requested when confidence is low, and guarded execution proceeds only with force and pose-deviation stops in place. In 180 real-robot trials, this system achieved 84% success compared with 69% for fixed overhead, 72% for fixed side, and 61% for open-loop execution. These results define evidence for one 7-DoF arm, one tabletop fixture, and one cylindrical peg family, without a statistical significance test, cross-robot evaluation, or deployment study.
