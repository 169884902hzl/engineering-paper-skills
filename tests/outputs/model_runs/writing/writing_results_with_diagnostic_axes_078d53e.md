In the tested tabletop occlusion setup, the confidence-triggered multi-view system ranks first among the evaluated variants, followed by the fixed side view, the fixed overhead view, and the open-loop controller. The strongest result is the full system's 84% success over 180 trials, which is 12 percentage points higher than the fixed side baseline, 15 percentage points higher than the fixed overhead baseline, and 23 percentage points higher than open-loop execution. The diagnostic pattern explains why the ranking is not simply a camera-placement effect: overhead viewing tends to fail when the peg hides the hole during final approach, side viewing improves lateral visibility but can still leave depth uncertainty unresolved, and open-loop execution mainly fails when pose errors are carried into contact without recovery. The full system improves this sequence by requesting another view when confidence is low and by using guarded execution after pose refinement. Its remaining failures, however, occur when all available views remain ambiguous or contact produces large pose deviation, so the result supports the tested system-level behavior without establishing statistical significance, component-level causal isolation, cross-robot robustness, or deployment readiness.
