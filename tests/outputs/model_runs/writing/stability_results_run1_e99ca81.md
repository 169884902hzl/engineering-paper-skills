Under partial visual occlusion, the confidence-triggered multi-view system produced the strongest insertion outcome, reaching 84% success over 180 trials, ahead of fixed side viewing (72%), fixed overhead viewing (69%), and open-loop execution (61%). The ordering follows the diagnostic failure modes rather than the camera labels: the overhead camera loses the hole during the final approach as the peg occludes it, whereas the side camera preserves more lateral evidence but still leaves depth uncertainty unresolved. Triggering an additional view when confidence is low therefore reduces the chance that guarded contact begins from an ambiguous pose estimate, while the contact execution stage limits the effect of residual pose error. By comparison, open-loop execution mainly carries unrecovered pose error into contact. The 12, 15, and 23 percentage-point margins over fixed side, fixed overhead, and open-loop execution, respectively, show that performance is governed by the interaction between visibility loss, depth uncertainty, and contact-pose recovery. The remaining failures occurred when all available views were ambiguous or contact produced a large pose deviation, defining the current scope of the single-arm tabletop evaluation with one cylindrical peg family.
