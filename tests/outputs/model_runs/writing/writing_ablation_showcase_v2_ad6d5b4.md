The ablation indicates that the main bottleneck in contact-rich action grounding is not merely executing a hinted motion, but verifying that a visually plausible hint is grounded to the intended insertion target. A naive direct action hint succeeds in 17% of trials, and adding geometry-aware execution raises success only to 23%, suggesting that execution geometry helps realize an action but cannot select the correct target by itself. Combining target-focused perception with geometry-aware execution increases success to 44%, showing that concentrating perception around the candidate target reduces scene-level ambiguity. The largest gain appears when overlay self-verification is added, lifting success from 44% to 76%; this delta attributes a central contribution role to rejecting poorly grounded action hints before contact. The full system reaches 88% with mask-constrained grounding, which further narrows spatial ambiguity near the target. Because the rows are additive and evaluated on one task family, these results support a contribution-level interpretation of the staged grounding pipeline rather than isolated causal or statistically significant component effects.
