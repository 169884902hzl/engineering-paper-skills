The ablation trend shows that the main gain comes from reducing perception and grounding failures before the action is executed, rather than from execution constraints alone. Starting from a naive direct action hint at 17% success, adding geometry-aware execution raises performance to 23%, a 6 percentage point increase that mainly supports more reliable action realization but does not by itself solve target selection. Adding target-focused perception raises success to 44%, indicating that scene-level ambiguity is a major bottleneck for choosing the relevant target. The largest improvement enters at the overlay self-verification stage: success increases from 44% to 76%, a 32 percentage point gain, consistent with its role in rejecting visually plausible but poorly grounded action hints before they reach execution. The final mask-constrained grounding step further raises success to 88%, supporting its role in reducing spatial ambiguity near the target. Because the study is additive rather than a fully isolated factorial design, these rows support contribution roles under one task family but do not prove independent causality, statistical significance, broad robustness, or deployment readiness.
