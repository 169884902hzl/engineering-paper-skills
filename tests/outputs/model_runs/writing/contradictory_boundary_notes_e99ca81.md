**Draft**

In the tabletop fixture study, the full system achieved 79% success over 90 trials, compared with 63% for the fixed-camera baseline. This 16-percentage-point gain suggests that the integrated sensing and execution pipeline can improve task performance under the tested tabletop conditions. The remaining failures also clarify the current operating boundary: shiny objects can still confuse pose estimation, and some grasps terminate when the force guard is triggered. Thus, the result supports a fixture-level improvement over the baseline, while indicating that reflective-object perception and guarded recovery behavior remain important constraints before the method can be treated as broadly deployable.

**Evidence Needed For The Stronger Claim**

To claim a general deployment-ready method, the paper would need cross-robot tests, outdoor or visually varied environments, industrial trials, confidence intervals or statistical tests, broader object/material coverage including shiny objects, and failure-recovery evidence for force-guard interruptions.
