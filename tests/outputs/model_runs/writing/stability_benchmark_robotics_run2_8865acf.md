The full system ranks first in the workbench-tray retrieval study, achieving 73% success over 150 trials, compared with 52% for proposal-only and 64% for proposal plus wrist-camera verification. The 12 percentage point gain from adding wrist verification suggests that rechecking tray-level proposals near the hand filters some proposal errors, particularly when reflective tool handles confuse the initial proposal stage. The remaining 9 percentage point gap between proposal-plus-verification and the full system indicates that verification alone does not cover execution-time failures: thin tools can pass the wrist check yet fail during lift, so the guarded lift check contributes a separate execution-stage screen before placement in the return bin. These results support the role of wrist verification as a perception correction step and the guarded lift as a downstream failure check. The evaluation is limited to two tray layouts and six tool categories, with no significance test, factory deployment, or multi-robot evaluation.
