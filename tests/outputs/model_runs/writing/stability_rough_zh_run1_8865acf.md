The insertion failure is driven less by the nominal peg-hole geometry than by how visibility changes as contact approaches: the overhead RGB-D view provides a clean initial estimate, but the peg increasingly occludes the hole, while the side view preserves lateral information with less reliable depth. We therefore use overhead and side RGB-D observations to estimate both pose and confidence, trigger an additional observation when confidence is low, and update the insertion pose before guarded execution. The guarded controller stops on force and pose-deviation limits, preventing early pose errors from being carried unchecked into contact. In a 180-trial evaluation, the full system reaches 84% success, compared with 69% using only the overhead view, 72% using only the side view, and 61% for open-loop execution. These results support the role of confidence-gated multi-view pose updates in the evaluated peg-in-hole setting, covering one 7-DoF arm, one fixture, and a cylindrical peg family.
