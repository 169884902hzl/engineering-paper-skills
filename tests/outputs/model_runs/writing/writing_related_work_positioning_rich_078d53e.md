Prior work relevant to contact-rich insertion can be grouped along three technical axes. Fixed-view insertion systems use overhead, side, or otherwise preselected sensing geometries to support repeatable manipulation under controlled setups [FixedViewInsertion], but the observation geometry is fixed before execution and cannot recover task-relevant visibility when the peg-hole relationship changes near contact. Active perception methods acquire additional views for manipulation or inspection [ActivePerceptionManipulation], yet the available positioning notes frame these methods primarily around object coverage or detection confidence rather than insertion-pose refinement coupled to guarded execution. Force-guarded insertion controllers detect unsafe contact and can stop or slow the motion [ForceGuardedInsertion], but they do not resolve the upstream visual pose ambiguity that may have produced the unsafe contact. The closest neighboring line combines perception with guarded insertion [PerceptionGuardedInsertion], but the distinction here is the same-loop combination of confidence-triggered view acquisition, insertion-pose refinement, and force/pose guarded execution. This positions the present pipeline as a bounded bridge between sensing, pose update, and guarded insertion, pending verified citations and protocols rather than a proven novelty claim.
