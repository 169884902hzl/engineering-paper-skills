Occlusion during the final approach, rather than initial visual recognition, is the central failure mode in our peg-hole insertion experiments. The overhead RGB-D view provides a useful global estimate early in the trial, but the hole becomes partially hidden by the peg near contact; the side view preserves lateral alignment cues while leaving depth less stable. Our full system therefore estimates insertion pose and confidence from the overhead and side views, requests an additional observation when confidence is low, updates the target pose, and executes insertion under force and pose-deviation guards. Across 180 real-robot trials, this strategy achieved an 84% success rate, compared with 69% for a fixed overhead view, 72% for a fixed side view, and 61% for open-loop execution. The ranking is consistent with the role of view updating in reducing the accumulation of early pose error before contact, while guarded execution limits the final insertion attempt to poses that remain within the tested tolerance. These results characterize a bounded tabletop setting with one 7-DoF robot arm and one cylindrical peg family, without statistical significance testing, cross-robot evaluation, or deployment-scale industrial validation.
