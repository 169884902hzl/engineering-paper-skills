# Tone and Risk

Use this for disagreement, missing experiments, and sensitive comments.

## Tone

- professional
- concise
- non-defensive
- evidence-linked
- appreciative without over-apologizing

## Disagreement

Structure:

1. acknowledge the concern
2. explain the scientific or scope reason
3. state manuscript clarification
4. include limitation if appropriate

Do not cite time, cost, convenience, or deadline as the primary reason for not
doing an experiment.

## Impossible or Out-of-Scope Requests

Safe pattern:

```text
We agree that [requested experiment] would further test [claim]. The current
study is limited to [scope], and the available evidence supports [bounded claim].
We have revised the manuscript to state this boundary explicitly.
```

## High-Risk Items

Flag to user before final response if:

- reviewer asks for a new central experiment
- requested analysis may change the main conclusion
- current evidence does not support a major claim
- line numbers or figure panels are unknown
- reviewers conflict
- appeal-like language appears

## Misunderstood Because Manuscript Is Unclear

If the reviewer is wrong but the manuscript made the misunderstanding possible,
revise first and then respond.

Pattern:

```text
We clarified in [section/figure/table] that [correct interpretation]. The
revision now distinguishes [misread point] from [actual method/claim].
```

Avoid:

```text
The reviewer misunderstood our method.
```
