# Learning from Neighboring Papers

Use this when extracting writing patterns from 3-5 strong papers.

## What to Learn

Learn section function and reader path, not wording.

For each paper, record:

- section opening job
- paragraph sequence
- where the gap is established
- where the method enters
- where formulas appear relative to prose motivation
- where baselines and metrics are defined
- how ablations map to claims
- where failures and limitations are placed

## Methods Extraction Card

| Paper | Overview defines what? | Subsection order | Formula before/after motivation | Execution/safety closure |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Experiments Extraction Card

| Paper | Evaluation questions | Setup compression | Result axes | Failure boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Related Work Extraction Card

| Paper | Technical axes | Nearest neighbor | Segment-ending gap | Bridge back to method |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Conclusion Extraction Card

| Paper | First sentence recovers what? | Hardest evidence retained | Limitation source | Future work restraint |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Anti-Copy Rule

If your notes contain mainly phrases, terms, model names, figure counts, or table
styles, you extracted the wrong thing. Rewrite the notes as reader-path rules.

## Bad Extraction Examples

- Copying a model-name list instead of learning how the paper groups methods.
- Counting figures and reproducing the same figure order without matching your
  evidence flow.
- Copying a citation-count pattern instead of identifying the nearest-neighbor
  distinction.
- Importing a strong claim from a stronger paper when your evidence is local.
